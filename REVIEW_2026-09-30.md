## Must fix before the course is presented

### 1. Connecticut now has AI rules, which undercuts the course's central premise
The memo, the curriculum ("A note on Connecticut's current posture"), and the faculty notes all say
that Connecticut courts have issued no AI-specific guidance. The faculty Q&A says "There's no
statewide rule requiring it." Those statements were accurate in mid-June. They're no longer accurate:

- **Practice Book § 4-9, "Generative AI Compliance"** took effect June 23, 2026, the day after the
  submission deadline. Anyone who uses generative AI to create or edit a court filing must
  independently verify all citations, legal authorities, and evidence it produced. Sanctions can
  include nonsuit or default.
- **§ 4-2(b), amended the same day:** signing a pleading certifies compliance with § 4-9.
- **Appellate § 62-6(d)** (July 14, 2026) adds the same certification. **§ 85-2** adds failure to verify
  AI output as a ground for sanctions.
- ***TOV Realty, LLC v. Suarez*, 355 Conn. 902 (2026):** the Connecticut Supreme Court sanctioned an
  attorney over roughly 7 unverified AI-generated citations and found a Rule 1.1 violation. The
  sanction was 6 additional CLE hours in ethics and law office management.
- **D. Conn.:** a $500 Rule 11 sanction for hallucinated citations. A separate judge warned in Aug. 2026
  that an "eye-catching sanction" may be needed.
- The CBA Ethics Committee itself still has **no** AI opinion. That part of the claim remains true.

This is good news for the course, not a problem. The opening can move from "there's no Connecticut
guidance" to "Connecticut just made verification a court rule, and here's the first Supreme Court
sanction." It's a much stronger hook than *Gencor*, which is still only an unresolved sanctions motion.
