## Description

> In 1884 the yacht Mignonette sank in the South Atlantic. Four men got into a 13-foot dinghy with two tins of turnips. On the twentieth day, the captain killed the 17-year-old cabin boy, Richard Parker, and the three survivors lived on his body until a ship found them. Back in England they told the authorities everything, sure that the "custom of the sea" was on their side. Two of them were sentenced to death for murder.
>
> We go through the court's own record of what happened in the boat, the judgment in R v Dudley and Stephens that still binds English courts, and the cases that have tested it since, from conjoined twins to hijacked planes. Then we ask: is killing to survive murder? We give three verdicts and tell you how sure we are.
>
