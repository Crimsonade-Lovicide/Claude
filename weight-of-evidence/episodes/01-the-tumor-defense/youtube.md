## Description

> A man's behavior changed overnight. Doctors found a tumor. They removed it and the urges
> stopped. When it grew back, they returned. So is he guilty?
>
> We go through the medical record, the law that decides insanity cases (written in 1843),
> and the most famous brain tumor in criminal history, the one found in the UT Tower
> shooter, and what the 1966 autopsy actually said. Then we give a verdict and tell you
> how sure we are.
>
> Content note: this episode discusses child sexual abuse and the 1966 University of Texas
> shooting. Nothing graphic is shown.
>
> Every 3D scene, scan and recreated document in this video is a reconstruction or
> illustration and is labelled on screen. The scan is not the patient's.

## Pinned comment

> Our verdicts: (1) he was responsible but far less so; he needed surgery and supervision,
> not prison (70%). (2) The UT Tower tumor deserves a footnote, not an acquittal (85%).
> (3) Neuroscience does not excuse everyone; what excuses is losing the capacity to respond
> to reasons (60%). Tell us which one you'd move, and what evidence would move it.
