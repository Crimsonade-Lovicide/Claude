# Research: Case 01, The Tumor Defense

**Question:** If a brain tumor made him do it, is he guilty?
**Bigger question:** All behavior comes from the brain. Does neuroscience eventually excuse everyone?

Tags: `RECORD` = in a primary source (paper, report, statute, opinion). `INFERENCE` = from
reliable secondary sources or our reasoning from the record. `UNCERTAIN` = sources
disagree or unverified; don't state on screen without the hedge shown.

Before air, spot-check every on-screen quote against the PDF scan or page cited.

likely amplified an interest he already had rather than creating one.

## B. The law

| # | Claim | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | M'Naghten (1843): M'Naghten shot Edward Drummond, Peel's private secretary, **believed** to have mistaken him for Peel (evidence not conclusive). He was acquitted as insane; Queen Victoria objected; the House of Lords put questions to the judges | INFERENCE | Wikipedia (Moran 1981) |

**Our reading (INFERENCE):** he knew it was wrong, so under M'Naghten he's sane. His only
legal path is the volitional ("couldn't control it") prong, which federal law and most
M'Naghten states dropped. By press accounts he was in Virginia, one of the three states
that pair M'Naghten with an irresistible-impulse test. His case was resolved without an
insanity trial; the paper describes a conviction and then a treatment-or-jail choice.

**Our reading (INFERENCE):** Whitman is the opposite of the Virginia case. His tumor fits
the story people want to tell, but the evidence is weak: no symptoms, no before-and-after,
and a pathologist who saw no link. Meanwhile months of planning point to deliberate choice.
The tumor's fame says more about our need for an explanation than about his brain.

## E. Ethics and handling notes

- The stepdaughter is a real victim. Mention her harm plainly; never make him sympathetic at
  her expense. No detail beyond "sexual advances" (the paper's wording).
- Whitman: say his name sparingly and show no photo of him. Name victims. Show no violence,
  no weapons, no reenactment. The tower appears at dawn, empty, as a reconstruction.
- Don't use the patient's MRI (journal copyright). Our scan is an illustrative rebuild, tagged.
- Quotes from 1966 documents keep Whitman's spelling on screen but are read cleanly.
