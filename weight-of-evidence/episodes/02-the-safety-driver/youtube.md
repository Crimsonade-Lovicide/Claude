## Description

> In 2018, a self-driving Uber test car hit and killed Elaine Herzberg in Tempe, Arizona. The car detected her 5.6 seconds before the crash and never worked out what she was. The safety driver was looking at her phone. Uber's factory emergency braking had been switched off. Only one person was ever charged.
>
> We go through the federal crash investigation (NTSB), the court records, and decades of research on what happens to human attention when a machine does the work. Then we ask the question that matters as robotaxis spread: when no one is at the wheel, who's guilty? We give three verdicts and tell you how sure we are.
>
> Content note: this episode discusses a fatal crash. Nothing graphic is shown, and no one involved is depicted.
>
> The road and vehicle are simplified reconstructions, and the sensor diagram is rebuilt from the NTSB's published timeline. All of them are labelled on screen.
