# Case 04: The Pernkopf Atlas

**Question:** Should surgeons use the Pernkopf Atlas?
**Runtime target:** 17–19 min. Narration about 2,800 words.
**Voice:** the same ElevenLabs voice and model as Cases 01–03.

Every factual line traces to `research.md` (IDs in brackets in the picture notes). Picture
notes name the tool that builds each shot:
- `3D:` Blender scene from `production/scenes.py`
- `GFX:` Pillow graphic from `production/gfx.py` / `production/ep04.py`

Narration is the `>` lines only. `production/narration.py` extracts them and applies the
pronunciation table at the bottom.

Care rules for this episode (research §F): **no plate is ever shown**, not cropped, blurred or
partial, and not the signatures, which sit on the plates. The atlas appears only as a closed,
untitled book (3D, tagged RECONSTRUCTION) or as its title set in type. No execution imagery, no
guillotine; the method is stated once (S28). We never say the atlas was "made from" the 1,377,
never call the people in it "Holocaust" or "camp" victims as a group, and never put a name next to
the atlas: no one in any plate has been identified. The 2014 patient is not named or described
beyond what STAT reported. Pernkopf gets no photograph (README rule 6). Batke is a "painter". The
Vienna Protocol is Rabbi Polak's ruling, not Yad Vashem's.

## I. THE ATLAS

### S10 · Act title
**Picture:** `GFX: title` I · THE ATLAS

### S11 · A book for students
**Picture:** `3D: book_closed` A thick, closed, untitled book on a dark table under a single key light; slow push. Tag RECONSTRUCTION. [A1, A2, B1]
> Eduard Pernkopf was born in 1888, in a village in Lower Austria. He studied medicine in Vienna and never left. In 1933 he became a full professor and head of one of the university's two anatomy institutes. That same year, he signed a contract with a Vienna publisher for an anatomy book for his students.

### S12 · The painters
**Picture:** `GFX: words` Four names stacked in mono: ERICH LEPIER · LUDWIG SCHROTT · KARL ENDTRESSER · FRANZ BATKE; sub "painters". [B5]
> To illustrate it, he hired painters: Erich Lepier, Ludwig Schrott, Karl Endtresser and Franz Batke. They painted from bodies dissected at his institute, one layer at a time.

### S13 · Four volumes
**Picture:** `GFX: timeline` 1937 · VOL. 1 / 1941 · VOL. 2 / 1952 · VOL. 3 / 1957–1960 · VOL. 4. [B2, B4]
> The first volume came out in 1937. The last was finished in 1960, five years after Pernkopf died. Four volumes, in seven books, with about eight hundred paintings.

### S14 · The standard
**Picture:** `GFX: card` ARGUMENT: "…the standard by which all other illustrated anatomic works are measured." D. J. Williams, *J Biomed Commun*, 1988. [B9, B10, B11]
> In the 1960s, a German editor rearranged the paintings into a two-volume atlas, published in English, Italian and Japanese, and later in Spanish and French. For decades, it was the atlas surgeons wanted. One historian of medical illustration called it the standard by which all others are measured.

### S15 · The signature
**Picture:** `GFX: card` RECORD: "…undisputed that Lepier repeatedly included a swastika in his signature." Senate Project, University of Vienna, 1998 (via Angetter, *Lancet* 2000). No plate, no signature image. [B6, B8]
> But some of the paintings carried something else. Next to his name, Erich Lepier sometimes painted a swastika. In later editions, the publisher airbrushed the symbols out. It missed a few.

### S16 · First weight
**Picture:** `3D: scale_tip_left` One pan drops. Lower third "EVIDENCE · A GREAT BOOK, SIGNED WITH A SWASTIKA". [B6, B11]
> So, the first weight on the scale. By almost every account, it's a great book, and at least one of the people who made it didn't hide what he believed. But a signature doesn't tell us how the book was made. For that, we need to know about the man who ran the institute, and the bodies it received.

## II. THE DEAN

### S17 · Act title
**Picture:** `GFX: title` II · THE DEAN

### S18 · March 1938
**Picture:** `3D: corridor` An empty institutional corridor, cold light. Lower third "VIENNA · MARCH 1938". Tag RECONSTRUCTION. [A4, A5]
> Pernkopf had joined the Nazi party in 1933, and soon after, its paramilitary wing, the SA. In March 1938, Germany annexed Austria. Within weeks, the university's dean of medicine was arrested and removed, and Pernkopf was put in charge of the faculty.

### S19 · The address
**Picture:** `GFX: document` Typed: "…die Ausschaltung der Erbminderwertigen, durch Sterilisation und andere Mittel." Translation beneath: "…the exclusion of the hereditarily inferior, by sterilisation and other means." Source "E. Pernkopf, address as dean, April 1938 · *Wien Klin Wochenschr* 1938;51:545". [A6, A7]
> In April 1938, he gave his first address as dean, in his SA uniform. In it, he called for, in his own words, the exclusion of the hereditarily inferior, by sterilisation and other means.

### S20 · The purge
**Picture:** `GFX: number` "153 OF 197" in ink; sub "teachers dismissed from the medical faculty, 1938 · most cited count". Source "E. Ernst, *Ann Intern Med* 1995". [A9, A10]
> His faculty was purged. More than half of its teachers were dismissed, most because they were Jewish. The most cited count is a hundred and fifty-three out of a hundred and ninety-seven. The historian Sabine Hildebrandt holds Pernkopf, as dean, personally responsible. Across the whole university that spring, about three hundred and fifty teachers and more than two thousand Jewish students were driven out.

### S21 · Rector, then prisoner
**Picture:** `GFX: timeline` 1943 · RECTOR OF THE UNIVERSITY / 1945 · REMOVED FROM EVERY POST / 1945–1947 · INTERNED NEAR SALZBURG. [A12, A13, A14]
> In 1943 he became rector of the whole university. When the war ended in 1945, he lost every post, and the American army held him in an internment camp near Salzburg until 1947. He was never charged with a war crime.

### S22 · The guest
**Picture:** `3D: book_closed` Night variant: the same book, the key light lower and warmer, dust in the beam. Tag RECONSTRUCTION. [A15, A1]
> The university never gave him his job back. But it let him go on working on the atlas, as a guest, in two borrowed rooms. He was still working on it when he died, in 1955.

### S23 · Second weight
**Picture:** `3D: scale_tip_right` The other pan drops. Lower third "EVIDENCE · THE MAN WHO MADE IT RAN A NAZI FACULTY".
> So, the second weight. This atlas wasn't made by a scientist who just happened to live under the Nazis. It was made by a committed Nazi who ran a faculty for them. That tells us what to think of him. Whether it changes what we think of the book depends on one thing: whose bodies were painted.

## III. THE BODIES

### S24 · Act title
**Picture:** `GFX: title` III · THE BODIES

### S25 · The question is asked
**Picture:** `GFX: timeline` 1985 · FIRST QUESTIONS IN PRINT / 1994 · HOWARD ISRAEL · WILLIAM SEIDELMAN / MARCH 1995 · YAD VASHEM WRITES TO VIENNA / 1996 · *JAMA*. [C3, C4, C5, C6]
> For forty years, almost nobody asked where the bodies came from. In the 1980s, a few doctors and historians started to. In 1994, Howard Israel, an oral surgeon at Columbia University, and the physician and historian William Seidelman took the question to Yad Vashem, Israel's Holocaust memorial. In March 1995, Yad Vashem wrote to the University of Vienna and to the publisher, asking for an inquiry.

### S26 · The commission
**Picture:** `GFX: words` "SENATE PROJECT · UNIVERSITY OF VIENNA" / "1997–1998". [C5, C7]
> At first, the universities said no victims could possibly be in the atlas. Then, in 1997, the University of Vienna set up a commission to find out. It reported in October 1998.

### S27 · At least
**Picture:** `GFX: card` RECORD: "…the true number of executed citizens must be higher than 1377." Senate Project (via Angetter, *Lancet* 2000). [C2, C8]
> Here's what it found. Under a decree of 1939, the bodies of people executed in Vienna went to the university's anatomy institutes. Between 1938 and 1945, Pernkopf's institute received at least thirteen hundred and seventy-seven. That counts only the ones confirmed by a death certificate. The commission said the real number was higher.

### S28 · Who they were
**Picture:** `GFX: card` RECORD, a typed list: "RESISTANCE · 'HIGH TREASON' · LISTENING TO ENEMY BROADCASTS · BLACK-MARKETEERING · PICKPOCKETING". Source "Senate Project (via Angetter, *Lancet* 2000)". [C9, C10, C11]
> Most of them were executed at the Vienna district court, by guillotine. More than half had been convicted of resistance or high treason. Others were put to death for black-marketeering, for pickpocketing, for listening to foreign radio. Eight of the executed were identified as Jewish. Seven of their bodies went to the institute.

### S29 · Not the camps
**Picture:** `GFX: words` "NO EVIDENCE OF BODIES FROM THE CAMPS" / sub "Mauthausen · Gusen". [C13]
> The commission found no evidence that bodies from the concentration camps reached the Vienna institute. So the people in question are not, as is often said, Holocaust victims as a group. They were people the regime sentenced to death, most of them for opposing it.

### S30 · Forty-one
**Picture:** `GFX: number` "41 OF 791" in ink; sub "paintings signed with dates from 1938 to 1945". Then card RECORD: "…it must be assumed that the models probably came from the group of 1377." [C15]
> Then the paintings. The commission counted seven hundred and ninety-one. Forty-one are signed with dates between 1938 and 1945. For those, it concluded, the models probably came from the executed. About three hundred and fifty can't be dated at all.

### S31 · Neither confirmed nor ruled out
**Picture:** `GFX: card` RECORD: "…can neither be confirmed nor negated." Senate Project. Then card RECORD: "…a considerable number of the paintings produced during and after the war are based on the bodies of these victims." Medical University of Vienna, 2021 ("it is to be assumed"). [C15, C16, C19]
> Whether any one painting shows an executed person, the commission said, can neither be confirmed nor ruled out. It made a list of every executed person handed to the institute, by name, but it could not match a single name to a single painting. In 2021, the Medical University of Vienna went further. It must be assumed, it said, that a considerable number of the paintings made during and after the war are based on the bodies of these victims.

### S32 · The registers
**Picture:** `GFX: words` "98 SPECIMENS · LABELLED FROM THE COURT · REMOVED FOR BURIAL", then "2019 · THE INSTITUTE'S BODY REGISTERS ARE FOUND" / sub "war-years entries not yet published". [C20, C21, C22]
> The commission also found remains of executed people still kept in the faculty's collections: nearly a hundred specimens labelled as coming from the court. They were taken out and buried. The institute's own register of bodies was thought to have been destroyed in an air raid in 1945. In 2019, most of it turned up in the anatomy library. The entries for the war years haven't been published yet. They may one day narrow the gap. They will probably never close it.

### S33 · Third weight
**Picture:** `3D: scale_tip_right` The pan sinks further. Lower third "EVIDENCE · SOME OF THEM ARE PROBABLY IN ITS PAGES". [C15, C16]
> So, the third weight, and the heaviest. We can't name anyone in this atlas. But it is very likely that some of the people in its pages were put to death by the regime and handed to the institute that made it, without anyone's consent. Every surgeon who opens it today opens it knowing that.

## IV. THE OPERATING ROOM

### S34 · Act title
**Picture:** `GFX: title` IV · THE OPERATING ROOM

### S35 · Out of print
**Picture:** `GFX: timeline` 1963–64 · ENGLISH EDITION / 1989 · THIRD EDITION / MID-1990s · PRINTING STOPS / 2019 · ORIGINALS GIVEN TO VIENNA. [B9, B10, B14, B15, B16]
> Once the questions started, the publisher stopped printing the atlas, in the mid-1990s. But the copies stayed on library shelves, and the images spread online. In 2019, Elsevier, which now owns the rights, gave the original paintings to Vienna's medical university.

### S36 · Who still uses it
**Picture:** `GFX: chart` Two bars: "NERVE SURGEONS · 2019 · 59% KNOW IT · 13% USE IT" / "ORAL AND FACIAL SURGEONS · 2021 · 49% KNOW IT · 9% USE IT". Sources *Neurosurgery* 2019; *Ann Anat* 2021. [D2, D4]
> It's still in use. In a 2019 survey of nerve surgeons, more than half knew the atlas, and thirteen percent were using it. Among oral and facial surgeons in 2021, nine percent.

### S37 · Once told
**Picture:** `GFX: number` "69%" in brass; sub "of nerve surgeons comfortable using it, once told its history · 2019". [D2]
> When the nerve surgeons were told its history, sixty-nine percent said they were still comfortable using it. When the authors offered conditions for using it ethically, three quarters of the rest agreed.

### S38 · Students
**Picture:** `GFX: chart` Three bars: "95% HAD NEVER HEARD OF IT" / "71% FOUND IT MORE COMPLETE" / "76% FOUND A MODERN ATLAS MORE USEFUL FOR LEARNING". Source "Coombs & Peitzman, *Ann Anat* 2017 · one US medical school". [D1]
> Medical students hardly know it. At one American medical school in 2017, ninety-five percent of the students who answered had never heard of it. Shown its pages next to a modern atlas, most found Pernkopf's more complete and accurate. But three quarters said the modern one was more useful for learning. Almost all of them wanted its history taught.

### S39 · The rabbi
**Picture:** `GFX: card` RECORD: "…their use would certainly be permitted by most authorities to help save lives (piku'ah nefesh)…" Rabbi Joseph A. Polak, the Vienna Protocol, November 22, 2017. [D8, D9]
> After the operation in St. Louis, Mackinnon and Yee asked whether they had been right to use it. The question went to Rabbi Joseph Polak, the head of a rabbinical court in Massachusetts, who as a small child survived Bergen-Belsen. In 2017 he answered. Normally, he wrote, Jewish law forbids taking benefit from the dead. But most authorities would certainly permit these drawings to be used to save a life.

### S40 · The condition
**Picture:** `GFX: card` RECORD: "…making it known to one and all just exactly what these drawings are. In this way, the dead are accorded at least some of the dignity to which they are entitled." Rabbi Joseph A. Polak, the Vienna Protocol, 2017. Small mono line beneath: "a rabbinic ruling, first presented at a Yad Vashem symposium; not a ruling of Yad Vashem". [D8, D9, D10]
> On one condition: everyone must be told exactly what these drawings are. That way, he wrote, the dead are given at least some of the dignity they are owed. His ruling is now known as the Vienna Protocol. It was first presented at a meeting at Yad Vashem, but it is his ruling, not the memorial's.

### S41 · Vienna's answer
**Picture:** `GFX: card` RECORD: "…far from irreplaceable." Then: "…nobody will reasonably object to such an ultima ratio." Czech, Druml, Weninger, Müller, Medical University of Vienna, *J Biocommun* 2021. [D14, D15, E3, E13]
> In 2021, Vienna's medical university, the successor to Pernkopf's own faculty, gave its answer. The atlas, it said, is far from irreplaceable. Its use should be cut to a minimum, and it should preferably no longer be used in teaching. But when it really is a patient's only or best hope, nobody will reasonably object. It now asks libraries to put a note about the atlas's history inside every copy. Not everyone agrees on the classroom. Sabine Hildebrandt once argued that using it with its history was not only justifiable but desirable. By 2019 she had stopped using it in her own lectures, and asks colleagues who do to explain where it came from.

### S42 · The steps
**Picture:** `GFX: words` A numbered list: "1 · STOP AND THINK / 2 · CALL A COLLEAGUE / 3 · TRY ANOTHER ATLAS / 4 · ONLY THEN, PERNKOPF". [D6, D7]
> Mackinnon's team now follows a set of steps. Stop and think. Call a colleague. Try another atlas. Only then, Pernkopf. And Mackinnon says that now, before she shows it to a patient, she tells them its history and asks for their consent.

### S43 · Not alone
**Picture:** `GFX: words` "SOBOTTA · CLEMENTE · SPALTEHOLZ" in dim mono, then "DACHAU · 1942" beneath. [B13, C24, D19, E6]
> Pernkopf's atlas isn't the only one. Some of its images were copied into other atlases, and another German atlas used images linked to executed prisoners and victims of the Nazi euthanasia killings. Scientists have also argued for decades over whether to cite the Nazi freezing experiments on prisoners at Dachau. But a review of those found the data unreliable. The bioethicist Arthur Caplan has put the wider point this way: not every use of Nazi-era information is morally out of bounds. Pernkopf's paintings are very good at what they do. That's what makes this case hard.

### S44 · Fourth weight
**Picture:** `3D: scale_tip_left` The first pan recovers some ground. Lower third "EVIDENCE · THE LINE IS DRAWN AT A LIFE". [D9, D14, D6]
> So, the fourth weight. Almost everyone who has thought hard about this, the rabbi, the university, the surgeons, ends up drawing the same line. In the operating room, to save a life or end real suffering, as a last resort, and out loud: yes. In the classroom, as just another textbook: no.

## V. THE VERDICT

### S45 · Act title
**Picture:** `GFX: title` V · THE VERDICT

### S46 · The case for using it
**Picture:** `3D: scale_tip_left` Lower third "THE STRONGEST CASE: USE IT". [E1, E2, E3, E4]
> First, the strongest case for using it. The people in it are already dead, and refusing to look helps none of them. If a page can end a patient's pain or save a life, choosing a worse outcome for a living person isn't respect for the dead. It's a cost someone else pays. Suppressing books is what the regime did. Better to use it, say where it came from, and let it teach two lessons at once.

### S47 · The case against
**Picture:** `3D: scale_tip_right` Lower third "THE STRONGEST CASE: LEAVE IT ON THE SHELF". [E7, E8, E9, E11, E12]
> Now the strongest case against. These people were killed by the state its author served, and their bodies were taken without anyone's consent. Every use draws benefit from that again. The atlas isn't irreplaceable, and most surgeons manage without it. And once you allow it to save a life, the exception spreads: to the lecture hall, to the slide deck, to the next textbook, until the history is gone and only the pictures are left. The patient in St. Louis wasn't told. That is what the exception looks like in practice.

### S48 · Verdict one
**Picture:** `GFX: verdict` Kicker IN THE OPERATING ROOM. VERDICT: "Use it as a last resort, when it can save a life or end real suffering, and tell the patient where it came from." CONFIDENCE 80%. WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND: "Evidence that other atlases or modern imaging now match it in the cases where surgeons still reach for it."
> Here's our verdict. In the operating room, use it, but only as a last resort, when it can save a life or end real suffering, and tell the patient where it came from. Refusing would put a living person's health below a gesture to the dead that they can't receive. Our confidence is eighty percent. What would change our mind: evidence that other atlases, or modern imaging, now match it in the cases where surgeons still reach for it. Then it would no longer be a last resort, and the case for using it would fall away.

### S49 · Verdict two
**Picture:** `GFX: verdict` Kicker IN THE CLASSROOM. VERDICT: "Don't teach anatomy from it. Teach its history instead." CONFIDENCE 75%. WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND: "Evidence that students taught from it, with its history, become more careful doctors, not less."
> In the classroom, don't teach anatomy from it. There are good alternatives, and a student doesn't need these paintings to learn where a nerve runs. Teach its history instead. Our confidence is seventy-five percent. What would change our mind: evidence that students who learn from it, alongside its full history, come away more careful doctors, not less. Some historians believe that, and if the evidence backed them, we'd move.

### S50 · Verdict three
**Picture:** `GFX: verdict` Kicker THE BIGGER QUESTION. VERDICT: "Knowledge taken from people a regime killed may be used to save a life, but never in silence." CONFIDENCE 70%. WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND: "If the people in it could be named, and their families asked that it not be used."
> And the bigger question. May we use knowledge taken from people who were killed and never agreed? Our verdict: we may use it to save a life, but never in silence. What we owe them is the truth, told every time. Our confidence is seventy percent, lower, because the people in this book can't speak for themselves, and we are deciding for them. What would change our mind: if the people in it could one day be named, and their families asked that it not be used. Their wishes should count for more than ours.
