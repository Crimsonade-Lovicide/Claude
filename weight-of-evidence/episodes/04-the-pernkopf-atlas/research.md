# Research: Case 04, The Pernkopf Atlas

**Question:** Should surgeons use the Pernkopf Atlas?
**Anchor:** Vienna, April 1938. Weeks after the Anschluss, the anatomist Eduard Pernkopf, new dean
of the medical faculty, gives his inaugural address in SA uniform and calls for the "exclusion of
the hereditarily inferior, by sterilisation and other means." Teachers are purged from his faculty.
During the war his institute receives the bodies of at least 1,377 people executed by the regime,
most for political reasons. His painters keep working on the *Topographische Anatomie des
Menschen*; some sign with a swastika. The atlas becomes the most admired in the world. In 2014 a
nerve surgeon in St. Louis, stuck in an operation, has a plate from it sent to the operating room
and finds the nerve. No one can say whose body that plate shows.
**Bigger question:** May we use knowledge made from people who were killed and never consented,
and if we may, what do we owe them each time we do?

Tags: `RECORD` = in a primary source (the Senate Project's published findings, a university's own
statement, a law or decree, a library catalogue record, a responsum, a survey's published results,
the period text itself). `INFERENCE` = reliable secondary sources or press, or our reasoning from
the record. `UNCERTAIN` = sources disagree or unverified; don't state on screen without the hedge
shown. Researched 21 Oct 2026.

Before air, spot-check every on-screen quote against the page cited. **The 1998 Senate report
itself is not online** (Hildebrandt's bibliography lists it as an "unpublished manuscript"); its
findings here come from Angetter's summary written "on behalf of the Senate Project" (Lancet 2000),
Malina & Spann (1999, abstract only) and the Medical University of Vienna's statements. Blocked to
us: Hildebrandt 2006 full text (Deep Blue behind a Cloudflare challenge; abstract only), the Lancet
Commission report and Krebs et al. 2024 (thelancet.com 403), PMC's PDF links (we read the JBC 2021
PDFs through the distantreader.org mirror of the journal's own site), Scimex (403), the JAMA 1996
letters and the Polak & Grodin 2018 Neurosurgery letter (not opened; known via citing papers). The
NYT 2020 piece was seen only in a Spanish-language syndicated reprint.

**Names.** Eduard Pernkopf (birthplace spelled Rappottenstein by Williams, Rapottenstein by the
University of Vienna). Erich Lepier. Franz Batke. Karl Endtresser (also "Entresser",
"Endtrasser"). Ludwig Schrott (Jr.; his father Ludwig Schrott Sr. also painted). Werner Platzer.
Alexander Pichler (Wikipedia "Pickler": wrong). Helmut Ferner. Gerald Weissmann (some "Weissman").
Howard A. Israel. William E. Seidelman. Sabine Hildebrandt. Susan E. Mackinnon. Andrew Yee. Rabbi
Joseph A. Polak. Michael A. Grodin. Camp Glasenbach = Camp Marcus W. Orr, near Salzburg. Polak
writes "Untermann Protocol" (one footnote "Unterman"). **We say Eduard Pernkopf, Erich Lepier,
Franz Batke, Karl Endtresser, Ludwig Schrott, Rabbi Joseph Polak, the Vienna Protocol, the Senate
Project.**

## F. Care rules

- **Never show a plate**, cropped, blurred or partial. The paintings are likely likenesses of
  people killed by the regime, and the show never depicts real victims. That includes the signature
  details, which sit on the plates. If we need the swastika signature, describe it, or set
  [ANGETTER]'s sentence on a card. (Rights: the Josephinum licenses images only "within the context"
  of named articles; Elsevier must approve more than three.)
- No Metzenbauer drawings of the institute's cadavers, no execution imagery, no guillotine. State the
  method once (C10) and move on. No dissection descriptions.
- **Words.** Say "people executed by the Nazi regime, most for political reasons" and "at least
  1,377 bodies delivered to the institute." Never "made from 1,377 bodies," never "concentration
  camp victims," never "Holocaust victims" for the plates as a group (C13, C15). Seven of the
  executed were Jewish; say that exactly.
- **No one in a plate has a name** (C19). Do not put any executed person's name or photo next to the
  atlas. If we honour named victims (README rule 6), only as "executed in Vienna and delivered to the
  anatomy institute," with the source, never "in the atlas."
- Pernkopf: README rule 6, don't linger. The April 1938 SA-uniform photos (ÖNB, ZG S 283) can carry
  one beat at most, licensed, or a text card instead.
- **Living patients:** do not name or depict the 2014 St. Louis patient or the Israeli boy the NYT
  named. Living scholars (Mackinnon, Yee, Hildebrandt, Polak, Grodin, Czech, Druml, Israel,
  Seidelman) appear as credited `ARGUMENT` cards, not characters.
- Don't call the Vienna Protocol a Yad Vashem ruling (D8). It is a rabbinic responsum presented at a
  Yad Vashem symposium.
- Batke's party membership is disputed (B6, B7): "painter," not "Nazi painter."
- "Best atlas ever" is never our claim; credit it (B11).
