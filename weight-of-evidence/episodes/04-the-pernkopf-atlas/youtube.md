## Description

> In 2014, a nerve surgeon in St. Louis couldn't find the nerve she was looking for. Her colleague sent a picture into the operating room, a page from an anatomy atlas made in Vienna between 1937 and 1960 by Eduard Pernkopf, a committed Nazi who ran the medical faculty for the regime. With it, they found the nerve. During the war, Pernkopf's institute received the bodies of at least 1,377 people executed by the Nazi regime, most of them for political reasons. No one in any of the atlas's paintings has ever been identified.
>
> We go through what the University of Vienna's own investigation found in 1998 and what is still unknown, how surgeons use the atlas today, the rabbinic ruling known as the Vienna Protocol, and the Medical University of Vienna's answer. Then we ask: should surgeons use it? We give three verdicts and tell you how sure we are.
>
> Content note: this episode discusses Nazi crimes and executions. No illustration from the atlas is shown, and no one involved is depicted.
