# Research: Case 05, The Golden State Killer

**Question:** Should police be allowed to search your family's DNA?
**Anchor:** Ventura County, California. In an evidence store sits DNA from the night in March 1980
when Lyman and Charlene Smith were killed in their home. For decades it matches no one: the man who
left it has never been arrested, so he is in no police database. In 2017 and early 2018 investigators
turn that DNA into the kind of file hobby genealogists trade, and upload it, under made-up accounts,
to websites where ordinary people have shared their DNA to find cousins. None of those people is the
killer. Some of them are his relatives. From their family trees, a small team works across the
generations until one man fits: a former police officer living in Citrus Heights, outside
Sacramento. On April 18, 2018, detectives swab the door handle of his car in a store car park; on
April 23 they take a tissue from his trash. On April 24 he is arrested. The relatives whose DNA
found him were never asked, and most still have not been named.
**Bigger question:** When one person shares their DNA, they share part of their relatives' genomes
too. Who gets to consent for a family, and what should the state be allowed to do with that?

## F. Care rules

- **Victims are people, not plot.** Name the thirteen murdered in full, with place and date (A5), and
  say something about each as a person where the record does (C12, C13). Do not use their deaths as a
  montage.
- **No description of violence or sexual assault.** State counts and charges plainly ("thirteen
  murders, nearly fifty rapes, 87 victims") and stop. Do not read from the factual-basis exhibits or
  the impact statements beyond the lines in C12. No crime-scene photos, no reconstructions of any
  attack, no "dark house at night" re-enactments.
- **Survivors.** The court record calls them Jane Does; keep it that way. Name a survivor only if she
  has spoken publicly under her own name (A18), and only with what she said in public.
- **The perpetrator.** Say his full name once, probably at the arrest beat; otherwise "the Golden
  State Killer," "he," or "the former officer." **Never show his photo** or the plea/sentencing video
  of him. Do not quote him (his "I'm truly sorry" at sentencing stays out). Do not use the People's
  jail-cell video (C11). He is not the story; the method is.
- **Living relatives.** Never name or hint at the relatives whose DNA was matched (the "Wright" pool,
  the Orange County cousin, her brother, the Oregon man and his daughter). No family-tree graphic with
  real names: the tree is a `RECONSTRUCTION` with blank or invented nodes, tagged
  `ILLUSTRATION · NOT DATA`.
- **Reconstructions.** Any DNA, database or tree animation is labelled. No re-created search screens
  that look like GEDmatch, FamilyTreeDNA or MyHeritage pages; use generic interface shapes.
- **No speculation as fact.** Which site broke the case is disputed (B8): say so. "The first time" is
  disputed (B18). Rape and burglary totals are unofficial (A11, A12).
- **Named living people** (Holes, Rae-Venter, Schubert, Moore, Rogers, Usry, Tapp, Carol Dodge,
  Murphy, Ram) appear as credited `ARGUMENT` or `RECORD` cards, not characters. Michael Usry and
  Christopher Tapp have spoken publicly; still, no photos without licence.

  "pleaded guilty to 13 rapes" (C3); "arrested April 25" (B13); "caught through GEDmatch" as the whole
