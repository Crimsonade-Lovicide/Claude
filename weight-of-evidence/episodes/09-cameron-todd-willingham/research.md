# Research: Case 09, Cameron Todd Willingham

**Question:** Should a state execute someone when the evidence is disputed?
(Owner's wording, kept. A sharper alternative, if the owner wants the question to name the mechanism:
*"Should anyone be executed on evidence science can no longer stand behind?"* See §J for why "disputed"
needs a threshold in the verdict, since almost every capital case is disputed by someone.)
**Anchor:** Corsicana, Texas, about 10:30 on the morning of Monday, December 23, 1991, two days before
Christmas. Smoke pours from the front door of a small one-story wood-frame house. A twenty-three-year-old father stands on the porch in a pair of jeans, his hair singed, shouting
that his babies are burning. His three daughters, Amber (2) and the twins Karmon and Kameron (1), die.
Within days a deputy state fire marshal reads the burned floor as a confession: pour patterns, puddles,
three points of origin, crazed glass, a charred threshold. Sixteen days after the fire the father is
arrested; eight months later a jury convicts him of capital murder after a two-day trial. Twelve years
later, on Friday, February 13, 2004, a fire scientist sends the parole board and the governor's office a
five-page report saying most of those conclusions "would be considered invalid in light of current
knowledge" and calling one of them an "Old Wives Tale." The board votes 15–0
against clemency; the governor declines a 30-day reprieve. On Tuesday, February 17, 2004, Texas executes
Cameron Todd Willingham. The independent fire scientists who have since reviewed the case, including one
hired by the State's own Forensic Science Commission, concluded that the evidence could not support a
finding of arson. The State has never overturned the conviction, never pardoned him, and its officials
have said he was guilty.
**Bigger question:** The law draws its line at innocence; this case sits in the space before it. Willingham
was never exonerated, and the people who knew him best are divided: his parents and cousin fought to clear
his name, and the children's mother, Stacy Kuykendall, says he confessed to her. What the record does show
is narrower and harder: the main physical evidence the State used to convict him did not show what the
jury was told it showed, a credible scientist said so before the execution, and the system had no
mechanism that required anyone to test that claim before the sentence was carried out. More than 200
people sentenced to death in the U.S. since 1973 have later been exonerated (DPI), and the best estimate is
that at least 4.1% of death sentences are false convictions (Gross et al., PNAS 2014). England abolished
the death penalty for murder in 1965. If a state keeps it, what must happen before it acts when the
evidence starts to fall apart?

## F. The science: what changed

| ID | Claim | Tag | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|

## J. Verdicts: candidates

Suggested verdict cards (Case 02 had three). **We rule on what the State knew and did, not on his guilt as a
fact we can't know.**

1. **THE FIRE EVIDENCE** (did the State's arson evidence show arson?). Candidate: *No. The indicators the jury
   heard were not evidence of arson, by today's science or by the best texts of 1991.* Confidence ~90%.
   What would change our mind: an independent fire scientist defending the specific indicators on modern
   science; surviving lab results showing accelerant in the bedroom or hall; documentation that accidental
   causes were actually tested and excluded.
2. **FEBRUARY 17, 2004** (should Texas have carried out the execution that day?). Candidate: *No. With a
   credible scientific challenge to the only physical evidence on the table, the State should have used the
   30-day reprieve to test it.* Confidence ~85%. What would change our mind: records showing the Board and
   the governor's office had the report in time, had a qualified fire scientist review it, and that reviewer
   found it unpersuasive on the merits; or a court that heard Hurst's claims on the merits before the
   execution.
3. **THE QUESTION** (should a state execute someone when the evidence is disputed?). "Disputed" needs a
   threshold or the answer is trivial. Candidate: *No state should carry out an execution while a credible,
   specific challenge to the evidence that convicted the person has not been heard and tested by someone
   competent to judge it.* Confidence ~80%. What would change our mind: evidence that such a rule is
   routinely exploited to delay executions without finding errors; or evidence that existing remedies (Texas's
   11.073 writ, conviction-integrity units, the SFMO review) now reliably resolve such challenges before
   execution dates.
4. Optional, handle with great care: **HIS GUILT** (can we say?). Candidate: *We can't know. Without the
   arson evidence, what remains would not prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt; that is not the same as
   proving him innocent.* Confidence that the remaining evidence falls short: ~75%. What would change our
   mind: a recording or corroborated record of the confession Stacy Kuykendall describes; independent
   corroboration of Webb's 1992 account; physical evidence of accelerant. (The owner may prefer to drop this
   card; the episode works on cards 1–3.)

## L. Widely repeated but wrong (do not say on air)

- **"Willingham was exonerated" / "proven innocent" / "Texas admitted it executed an innocent man."** No court,
  board or official has said so. The TFSC expressly made no finding on guilt or innocence, and its addendum
  made no negligence finding (D11, D14).
- **"The Forensic Science Commission found the arson finding was wrong."** It made no finding on the cause or on
  negligence; it said the indicators' value had changed, called the SFMO's "stand by" position "untenable,"
  and issued 17 recommendations (D11–D14). Beyler, its consultant, is the one who wrote "A finding of arson
  could not be sustained."
- **Beyler said there was "no credible evidence" of arson / called it "junk science" / "slipshod."** Those
  words are press paraphrase; none appears in his report (we searched the text). Quote D6.
- **"The Board and Perry never received / ignored Hurst's report."** It was faxed to both on Feb 13 (TFSC);
  Perry's office says he was briefed. What is undocumented is what anyone did with it (C7–C10).
- **"The Board voted 15–0 after reading Hurst."** At least four of the fifteen voted before it was signed; for
  the rest, unknown (C8).
- **"The fire was an accident."** The defensible conclusion is that arson was not shown and the cause is now
  undeterminable (F7). Hurst thought an accident likely; that is his opinion.
- **"The only evidence was the arson science."** The jury also heard Webb, neighbors, his record and the
  sociopath testimony (B2–B6). The arson finding was the core.
- **"Webb lied in exchange for a deal" / "Jackson made a secret deal."** Webb has given several accounts; a
  jury cleared Jackson 11–1 in 2017 (E7–E8).
- **"Vasquez said 'the fire does not lie' to the jury as his own invention."** The TFSC records that the phrasing
  was standard in training materials of the day (§K).
- **"Stacy testified against him."** At trial she testified for him at sentencing, and her family testified on
  both sides of the venue motion ([CCA95]; [AP09]). Her view changed later.
- **"Perry fired the whole commission."** He replaced three or four members, including the chair (counts differ,
  D7), saying their terms had expired.
- **"The fire happened in Huntsville."** Corsicana (the Texas Tribune timeline's error); Huntsville is where he was
  executed.
- **"Grann's article proved the State's case was invalid."** It is reporting; cite the primary reviews.
- **"Scalia said no innocent person has ever been executed."** He said the dissent did not discuss "a single
  case—not one—in which it is clear" (G4). Quote him exactly, including "by the abolition lobby."
- **"Willis was pardoned."** He was released, the indictment dismissed, and he was compensated as actually
  innocent; a gubernatorial pardon isn't in what we read (H1).
- **"Art. 11.073 / the commission came too late only because of politics."** Both postdate the execution; the
  commission didn't exist until Sept 2005 (D5, G7).
